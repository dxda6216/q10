How this calculator works

What Q10 means

Q10 is the factor by which a rate changes for a 10 °C rise in temperature. From rates R1 and R2 measured at temperatures T1 and T2:

Q10 = (R2 / R1)10 / (T2 − T1)

Typical enzyme-catalysed reactions have Q10 of about 2 to 3. For a circadian clock, the “rate” is its frequency, 1/period. Circadian clocks are temperature-compensated, so their period changes only a little with temperature and Q10 is usually close to 1 (roughly 0.8 to 1.2).

Q10 > 1Period shortens as temperature rises (the clock runs faster).
Q10 = 1Perfect temperature compensation.
Q10 < 1Period lengthens as temperature rises (overcompensation).

The model that is fitted

With τb the period at the base temperature Tb:

τ(T) = τb · Q10(Tb − T) / 10(1)

The frequency, in cycles per day, is f(T) = 24 / τ(T). Taking logarithms gives a straight line in T with slope ln(Q10)/10:

ln f(T) = ln(24 / τb) + ((T − Tb) / 10) · ln Q10(2)

The base temperature

The base temperature only decides at which temperature the fitted period τb is reported. Q10, its standard error, R² and the fitted curve are the same whatever base you choose; only τb and its error change. A base far outside your data, such as absolute zero (−273.15 °C), makes τb a long extrapolation with a large error.

The three plots

PeriodPeriod (h) against temperature (°C), with the fitted curve from model (1).
FrequencyFrequency, 24/period (day−1), against temperature.
Arrheniusln(frequency) against 1000/T, where T is in kelvin.

The Arrhenius equation k = A · exp(−Ea / RT) gives a straight line of ln k against 1/T with slope −Ea/R (Ea is the activation energy, R = 8.314 J mol−1 K−1). Model (2) is linear in T rather than 1/T, so the fitted curve is slightly bent on the Arrhenius plot. The two descriptions are approximately related by

Ea ≈ R · T1 · T2 · ln(Q10) / 10   (T1, T2 in kelvin)

In every plot, orange points are used in the fit, light-green points are outside the chosen temperature range, and the dashed line is the fitted Q10 curve.

Arrhenius regression line

The Arrhenius plot can also show the ordinary straight-line regression of ln(frequency) on 1000/T (solid grey line), fitted to the same data used for Q10:

ln f = intercept + slope · (1000 / T)   Ea = −slope · 1000 · R

Its Ea, slope, intercept, standard errors and R² are listed in the report. The two lines come from different models and are fitted to different residuals (period for Q10, ln f for the regression), so they nearly coincide within your data but separate slightly beyond it. Turn the line on or off with Show Arrhenius regression line.

How the fit is calculated

  1. Objective. Non-linear least squares on the period itself (not its logarithm), minimising SSE = Σ (τi − τ̂(Ti))². This is the same objective as SciPy’s curve_fit in the original Python program, so the results agree.
  2. Starting values. A straight-line regression of ln(period) on temperature, converted to τb and Q10 through equation (2).
  3. Optimisation. The Levenberg–Marquardt method, working on ln τb and ln Q10 so both stay positive. Each step solves (JᵀJ + λ·diag(JᵀJ))·δ = Jᵀr; λ shrinks after a step that lowers SSE and grows after one that doesn’t.
  4. Standard errors. From the covariance matrix s² · (JᵀJ)−1 with s² = SSE / (n − 2), where J is the Jacobian with respect to τb and Q10. The ± values are the square roots of its diagonal.
  5. Goodness of fit. R² = 1 − SSE / SST, where SST = Σ (τi − mean τ)².
  6. Fitted curve. Drawn with 200 points across the data’s temperature range, extended on each side by a third of its width.

How to use it

  1. The page opens with a demo calculation of example data. Click Clear to start with your own.
  2. Enter one temperature (°C) and period (h) pair per line, separated by commas, spaces, tabs or semicolons. Or use Load CSV… with temperature in column 1 and period in column 2. A header line and lines starting with # are skipped.
  3. To fit only part of the data, tick Use only data within a temperature range and set Low and High (both ends included).
  4. Choose the base temperature. Minimum, Maximum, Average and Median are taken from the data used in the fit.
  5. Click Calculate, or press Ctrl+Enter (⌘+Enter on a Mac).
  6. Switch between the Period, Frequency, Arrhenius and Report tabs.
  7. Save figure saves all three plots stacked in one PNG or SVG image. Save report saves the results as a text file; tick Also show tab-delimited data to include data you can paste into a spreadsheet.

Privacy and offline use

  • Calculations run in your browser. The fit, the plots and the report are all computed by the JavaScript in this file on your own computer.
  • Nothing is sent. The data you enter and the results are never sent to any server. The page contains no code that uploads, transmits or reports anything.
  • Nothing is stored. The page does not use cookies or browser storage, and it asks the browser not to remember form contents. Your data and results disappear when you close or reload the page. Files are written only when you click a Save button, and only to the place you choose.
  • The only outside request is for fonts. With an internet connection, the browser downloads the page’s typefaces from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com). That request contains none of your data, but like any web request it lets Google see your IP address and browser type.
  • Works offline. Once you have the HTML file on your computer, you can open it and calculate without an internet connection. Only the typefaces change: the page falls back to your system fonts.
  • Download. The HTML file is available from github.com/dxda6216/q10/blob/main/index.html. On that page, click the “Download raw file” button, then open the saved file in your browser. Following this link is the only other way the page connects to the internet, and only when you click it.
